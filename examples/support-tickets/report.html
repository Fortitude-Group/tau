Synthetic support tickets: how urgent is this ticket?

Synthetic data Every item in this dataset was generated, not written by real customers. Treat the numbers as a demonstration of the method, not as evidence about real traffic.

This report measures how far 5 model(s) can be trusted on "How urgent is this customer support ticket?", using synthetic tickets data (1,000 held-out items). Reference: claude-opus-5-5's answers, not the dataset's labels. The question here is whether a local model can stand in for the frontier call, and whether its confidence says when, so every rate below is agreement with the frontier model. Out of the box, laya-en agrees with claude-opus-5-5 on 23.2% of held-out items with a calibration error (ECE) of 0.273; after calibration its ECE is 0.155, 43.4% lower, short of the 50% goal. At a 20.0% target disagreement, jev-1.13 keeps 39.8% of decisions local, the most of any model (laya-en, the least, keeps 0.1%); the cascade's served answers agree with claude-opus-5-5 on 89.3% (the frontier alone agrees with itself by construction), at an estimated £614 per million decisions against £997 (list prices, 2026-09-27). The dataset's own labels are reported as a finding, not used: claude-opus-5-5 agrees with them on only 23.8% of 1,000 items, and a secondary table shows every model against them.

The misses

  • Calibration cut laya-en's held-out ECE by only 43.4% (from 0.273 to 0.155), short of the 50% goal.
  • Why laya-en fell short: the calibrator is chosen by lower calibration-split log loss, fixed before any held-out result. That picked isotonic (log loss 1.422, ECE 0.216) over temperature (log loss 1.581, ECE 0.016). Log loss and max(p) ECE disagree here, and the rule was not changed after seeing the held-out result.
